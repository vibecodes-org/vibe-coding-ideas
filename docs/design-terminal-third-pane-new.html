Terminal side by side — the third pane

What changes, in one page. Fixes “a third terminal goes missing instead of getting its own pane”.
The rule: side by side works for 2 or 3 terminals — never more. While it's on, every open terminal has its own pane, and every tab sits exactly above its own pane. The moment that can't be true (a 4th terminal, or the window gets too narrow), the whole thing switches to plain tabs — no panes at all — and a message tells you why. It switches itself back the moment 3-or-fewer fit again.

1 · Three terminals, three panes, three tabs — lined up

The tab row and the pane row are built from the same layout, so each tab's left and right edges are its pane's edges. The dashed lines are annotation only — they run through both rows to show the alignment.

Exactly as many tabs as panes — always. One pane owns the keyboard, shown four ways at once: sky border and glow, “⌨ Typing here” vs “◇ Watching”, brighter header, solid blinking cursor vs hollow one. Ctrl+Shift+←/→ steps the keyboard one pane left or right (it stops at the ends, no wrap-around). The two small buttons at the top-right (back to tabs, new terminal) stay in their usual corner.

How you get here: if you're already side by side with 2 terminals and you open a third, it simply gets its own pane on the right and the keyboard moves to it — that's the fix. If you turn side by side on while 3 terminals are open, all three get panes at once. If the window is too narrow for three panes at that moment, it stays as tabs and tells you why (“Not enough room for a third pane — switched to tabs. Side by side returns when the window is wider or a terminal closes.”).

2 · Opening a 4th terminal — everything becomes tabs

Four terminals never fit side by side. The instant a 4th one opens, all panes disappear and the dock becomes plain tabs — the same tabs you have today — with the new terminal at the front and a message explaining what happened.

Nothing is lost — all four terminals keep running, they're just tabs now. The side-by-side button stays lit (your preference is remembered) and its tooltip says when it comes back. Close any terminal so 3 remain and the three panes return by themselves — no button to press. The same message-and-return pattern covers a too-narrow window: too narrow for the current number of panes → tabs; wide enough again → panes come back.

3 · A popped-out terminal — never one of the lined-up tabs

A terminal you've popped out into its own window doesn't have a pane, so it can't have one of the lined-up tabs either. Instead it becomes a small purple ⧉ badge in the top-right corner, next to the other buttons — click it to bring that window to the front. It doesn't count toward the 3.
