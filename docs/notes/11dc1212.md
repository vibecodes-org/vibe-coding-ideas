### Where the baseline comes from
The earlier plan on card 4a95de65 (board d650f96a, Plan step ac2653af). 14 days of laptop helper transcripts (every board):
621 helper runs; 205 rebuilds caused by the helper's own waiting (about 0.33 per run, about $90 at
list price); 117 more caused by the coordinator resuming a finished helper (out of scope).
The dollar figures are list-price yardsticks; the real gain is allowance headroom.

### What I decided
1. **Carry the rule in the persona text VibeCodes delivers, not in the coordinator's instruction.**
   Add a fixed "Waiting on slow work" section at delivery time, the same way the read-only
   "## Tools" note is added (`agentPersonaBody` in `src/lib/agent-tool-access.ts`). It is never stored.
   Why: the helper's own system prompt is the only text that reliably reaches the helper. The
   claim `instruction` goes to the coordinator, which writes the brief itself. On master that
   instruction is size-pinned (`claim-instruction.test.ts`, at most 3,500 characters) and is being
   trimmed on purpose. One function feeds every route: claim `persona_prompt`,
   `get_agent_prompt(as_persona)`, the in-app `--agents` definitions and the `sync_board_agents` files.
2. **Claude helpers only in stage 1.** The wording names Claude Code tools (Bash timeout,
   run_in_background, Monitor). Codex's cache works differently and has no measured baseline.
   The agent kind is already passed through this way for the auto-compact window (`agent === "claude"`).
3. **Every Claude helper on every board, all roles.** The rule only applies while waiting, so it costs nothing otherwise.
4. **No hook in stage 1.** Stage 2 is a blocking check, added only if the 3-day check shows long waits still happening.
5. **Measuring:** (a) a small read-only transcript scan on the laptop (aggregates only), and
   (b) the board's own recorded per-step usage (`workflow_step_usage`: cache-write vs cache-read
   tokens per call) as a cross-check anyone can read.

### Ruled out
- Putting the rule in the claim `instruction` ("include this in the brief"). It depends on the coordinator
  copying it, it adds to a size-pinned block, and the coordinator would keep that text in its context for the whole session.
- Editing the five personas by hand (the earlier plan on card 4a95de65). It misses other and future agents and
  risks clobbering persona fields; the delivery note covers all of them.
- The browser launch prompt. Its length budget is tight (`MAX_LAUNCH_URL_LENGTH`) and it reaches the coordinator, not helpers.
- 1-hour cache, a cheap "wait for CI" step, GNU `timeout`: see the earlier plan on card 4a95de65 (same reasons).
